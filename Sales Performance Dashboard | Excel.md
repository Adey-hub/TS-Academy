# Sales Performance Dashboard | Excel

## About the Project

I built this Sales Performance Dashboard in Microsoft Excel to turn a transactional sales dataset into a clear and practical view of business performance.

The starting point was a dataset containing **2,098 sales transactions recorded between January and May 2025**. Rather than looking at the transactions individually, I wanted to bring the data together in a way that makes it easier to understand where sales are coming from, which areas of the business are performing well, and where there may be opportunities for improvement.

The dashboard therefore brings together financial performance, product sales, customer information, geographical performance, sales channels, and sales representative performance in one place.

## A Quick Look at the Data

The five-month period generated **₦2.328 billion in revenue**, with **₦1.863 billion in Cost of Goods Sold (COGS)** and **₦465.674 million in profit**. A total of **11,488 units** were sold across the 2,098 transactions.

The sales data covers four regions, namely **North, South, East, and West**, and four cities: **Lagos, Abuja, Kano, and Port Harcourt**.

The product portfolio consists of **10 products** grouped into three categories: **Furniture, Home Appliances, and Electronics**. The data also distinguishes between **Retail and Corporate customers** and records sales through two channels, **Online and Store**.

There are also six sales representatives whose performance can be compared through the dashboard.

## What I Did With the Data

My approach was to move beyond simply presenting totals. I structured the analysis around the questions that a business stakeholder would naturally want to answer.

I looked at how sales changed over the five-month period to understand the overall trend. I compared regions and cities to see where sales were strongest. I analysed products and categories to identify the areas contributing most to revenue, sales volume, and profit.

I also examined customer types and sales channels to understand how different groups contribute to overall performance. Finally, I included sales representative performance to provide another perspective on the people driving the sales results.

The intention was to make the dashboard useful not only for reporting what happened, but also for helping the user think about **why it happened and where attention may be required**.

## Key Areas Covered

The dashboard provides a view of:

**Overall Sales Performance:** Revenue, COGS, profit, and units sold.

**Monthly Performance:** How sales and profitability changed from January through May 2025.

**Regional and City Performance:** Comparison of sales performance across the four regions and major cities represented in the dataset.

**Product and Category Performance:** Identification of products and categories contributing to sales and profitability.

**Customer Analysis:** Comparison between Retail and Corporate customers.

**Sales Channel Analysis:** Comparison of Online and Store sales.

**Sales Representative Performance:** Assessment of the contribution of individual sales representatives.

## Building the Dashboard

I used Microsoft Excel to clean, organise, analyse, and visualise the data. The dashboard was developed from the underlying transaction-level sales data, using Excel's analytical and visualisation capabilities to create summary metrics and interactive views.

The focus throughout the process was on keeping the dashboard simple enough to understand at a glance while still providing enough detail to support deeper analysis.

I also paid attention to the relationship between the key financial measures. Revenue alone does not necessarily tell the full story of performance, so COGS and profit were considered alongside sales volume to provide a more balanced view of the business.

## Why This Dashboard Matters

One of the main lessons from working on this project is that having a large amount of sales data does not automatically make it useful. The real value comes from organising the data around meaningful business questions and presenting the results in a way that people can understand and act on.

This dashboard is intended to provide that bridge between **raw sales transactions and business decisions**.

A manager can use it to quickly identify strong-performing regions, products, channels, customers, or sales representatives, while an analyst can use the underlying data to investigate the reasons behind those results.

## Final Note

This project was an opportunity to take a real-world style sales dataset and turn it into something that tells a story.

The numbers in the dashboard represent more than just sales totals. They provide a way to understand **what is selling, where sales are coming from, who is buying, which channels are performing, and how these activities translate into revenue and profit**.

The dashboard is therefore not intended to replace detailed analysis. Rather, it serves as a starting point for asking better questions, identifying patterns, and making more informed business decisions.
